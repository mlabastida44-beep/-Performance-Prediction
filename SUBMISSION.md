### How to discuss the metrics (use your own numbers)
- **Accuracy** is the overall share of correct predictions, but about one third of students are at risk, so accuracy alone can hide weak performance on that class.
- **Recall (AT RISK)** matters most here: a missed at-risk student (false negative) means a learner who needed help is not flagged.
- **Precision (AT RISK)**: false positives mean extra support offered to students who may not need it, a cheaper error than a miss.
- G1 and G2 dominate feature importance, so the model is strong mostly because earlier grades predict later grades; it is less useful for very early warning.
- Possible improvements: cross-validation, tune threshold below 0.5 to raise recall, try another model, remove G1/G2 for earlier prediction.

## E. Reflection (draft answers; edit in your own words)
1. **Why must G3 not be an input?** It defines the label. Using it is target leakage: the model would "know" the answer and show unrealistic accuracy that fails on real new students.
2. **Why a Pipeline?** The same imputation, scaling and encoding is applied at training and prediction time, avoiding inconsistencies and preventing test-data leakage.
3. **Why is accuracy not enough?** Classes are imbalanced (~1/3 at risk); recall for the AT RISK class shows how many struggling students are actually caught.
4. **Ethical concerns:** labelling can stigmatize students; dataset includes sensitive attributes; model may be biased or wrong; predictions must support, not replace, teacher judgement.
5. **Limitations:** single school and country, small sample, only one train/test split, older data.
6. **Link to SAD:** Problem → requirements (upload/predict/export) → design (pipeline + UI) → implementation (Python/Streamlit/GitHub) → testing (T1-T6).
